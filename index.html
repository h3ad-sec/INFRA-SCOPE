THE PROCESS

When you hit SCAN, nothing gets routed through some black-box server in the middle. Your own browser does the calling, straight to each data source using your own key, or through a small stateless relay for the handful of vendors that block browser requests outright. Nothing is logged or kept in either path. Here's what actually happens to your target between the moment you submit it and the moment sixteen panels are sitting in front of you.

1
Resolve
Your domain or IP gets resolved to a real address first, using DNS, the system that turns a name like example.com into the number computers actually route traffic to. Everything downstream works from that confirmed address, not a guess.
2
Attribute
That address gets checked against the official internet routing registries, the same records ISPs themselves rely on, to find out who actually owns it: which network, which organization, roughly where in the world.
3
Enumerate
WHOIS history, certificate transparency logs, and passive DNS get cross-checked to build out everything connected to the target: subdomains, past infrastructure, the registration paper trail behind it.
4
Fingerprint & correlate
Open ports, running services, TLS fingerprints, and CDN/WAF detection show what's actually live and what's shielding it. Threat intel checks the target and everything found around it against known-malicious indicators, tying all sixteen panels back to one scan.

HOW TO USE

  1. Add your keys: click 🔑 KEYS in the nav, paste your source API keys, click SAVE KEYS. Sources with no key are simply skipped, everything else still runs.
  2. Set your target: type or paste a domain or IP into the target bar.
  3. Hit SCAN: every panel populates independently as its sources respond, no waiting on the slowest one.
  4. Drill in or export: open any panel for the full breakdown, export when you need it.
